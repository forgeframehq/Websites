Positioning
Recommended angle
Build a free PDF toolkit that processes files in the browser. The files never leave the person's device, and there is no account, no daily or hourly task cap, and no watermark. Lead every headline with that promise, not with a generic \"all the PDF tools you need\" line that iLovePDF and Smallpdf already own.
Three angles were compared. The first, in-browser privacy, is the clearest product gap. A 15 Nov 2026 comparison of nine major web toolkits (https://trencada.com/pdf-tools-benchmark-2026/) found that all nine upload files to servers for their web tools, and that none processed files in the browser on the free tier. iLovePDF, PDF24, and Sejda offer local processing only through installed apps, often with paid or Windows-only constraints. In-browser tools exist, but they are still a smaller category than the upload-based incumbents (see https://imisspdf.com/blog/10-in-browser-pdf-tools-no-upload-2026/).
The second angle, no sign-up and no task caps, is necessary but not enough on its own. PDF24 already claims unrestricted free use, funded by ads, while still uploading files. Smallpdf's two-conversions-per-day cap, Adobe's one-free-transaction rule, and Sejda's three-tasks-per-hour rule are real pain points, and autocomplete plus rising searches are full of \"free no sign up\" variants. Combine this generosity with the privacy architecture, rather than trying to out-PDF24 PDF24 on \"unlimited\" alone.
The third angle is use-case and target-size pages: compress to 1 MB or 200 KB, compress for email, sign or merge on iPhone, job-application file caps. These should be product features and content, not the only brand story.
Working line, until a name is chosen: free PDF tools that run in your browser. Your files never leave your device. No account, no limits, no watermark. Be honest on pages where quality is hard (PDF to Word, PDF to Excel, OCR): ship a useful v1, state limits, and never copy competitor accuracy claims.

Positioning
Every tool you need, in one place, described as 100% free and easy. Premium is sold for limits, apps, workflows, and an ad-free experience.
Tool set
Broad suite: merge, split, compress, Office and image conversions, edit, sign, watermark, rotate, unlock, protect, organize, PDF/A, repair, page numbers, scan, OCR, compare, redact, crop, forms, plus AI summarize and translate, and PDF to Markdown. URL pattern
Mostly root-level underscore slugs, such as /merge_pdf, /compress_pdf, /pdf_to_word, /split_pdf, /unlock_pdf.
Title pattern
Action-first, often with free or online: "Merge PDF files online. Free service to merge PDF"; "Compress PDF online. Same PDF quality less file size"; "PDF to WORD | Convert PDF to Word online for free".
Meta pattern
Short benefit plus "online, easily and free." Homepage: "iLovePDF is an online service to work with PDF files completely free and easy to use. Merge PDF, split PDF, compress PDF..."
Free limits
No single daily cap is published. Limits are per task. A 28 Oct 2026 read of the pricing comparison (cited in https://techpresso.co/reviews/ilovepdf-review) listed free caps such as merge 25 files / 100 MB, compress 2 files / 200 MB, PDF to Word and OCR 1 file / 15 MB, image to PDF 20 / 40 MB, edit 1 / 100 MB, sign 3 / 50 MB. Confirm on https://www.ilovepdf.com/pricing before launch, because the pricing page itself is table-heavy and changes.
Where files go
Web uploads go to iLovePDF servers (EEA by default) and are deleted within 2 hours, or immediately with Delete now. Desktop processes locally. Signed documents are kept longer (stated as 5 years) for audit trails. Source: https://www.ilovepdf.com/help/security
Weaknesses to exploit
Free use still uploads the file; free conversion and OCR caps are tight (15 MB / one file); ads on free; Premium is required for desktop, workflows, and higher limits. Homepage says tools are 100% free, which is easy to contrast with the paid limit table.
Paid
Premium from $5 / month billed annually or $9 billed monthly, as shown on the pricing page when fetched on 26 Nov 2026.

On-page outline
Open with the uploader and a live output-size readout. Explain that compression runs on the device. Offer quality presets plus a target-size control (1 MB, 500 KB, 200 KB) because autocomplete is full of those queries. How-to: add PDF, pick a target or preset, compress, download. Short section on how compression works (images and fonts, not magic). Use cases: email attachments, application portals, sharing over chat. FAQ: will quality drop; is it free; does the file get uploaded; how to hit 1 MB; scanned PDFs. Related: compress to 1 MB, merge, PDF to JPG, split. Shared on-page pattern for every tool: unique title, meta, and H1; a working widget above the fold; a one-sentence privacy promise; 3 or 4 how-to steps; options or limits; 4 to 6 FAQs in visible HTML; a related-tools row; no invented testimonials, user counts, or accuracy percentages.
Technical SEO checklist
Status as of this brief, 26 Nov 2026 (Atlantic/Bermuda): the site is not built, so every item is still outstanding. Claude should implement these as part of the first build, not as a later cleanup.
Core Web Vitals: LCP from a lightweight hero and heading, not from the PDF engine. Lazy-load pdf-lib, pdf.js, or WASM after first paint. Code-split per tool. Avoid layout shift in the uploader. Keep INP snappy on large files with web workers.
Mobile-first layout and usable tool UI on iPhone and Android browsers, including file pickers and signature drawing. Many long-tail queries are device-specific.
Render indexable HTML for titles, H1, how-to, FAQ, and internal links. Use static generation or SSR for page chrome and content. The PDF engine itself can be client-only.
XML sitemap at /sitemap.xml covering homepage, /tools, every tool page, and every published guide. Submit it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools on launch day.
robots.txt allowing those URLs, blocking only true utility paths (preview blobs, internal APIs). Do not block CSS or JS that Google needs to render the page.
Self-referencing canonical on every indexable page. HTTPS only, trailing-slash rule chosen and redirected consistently, one host (www or apex, not both).
Schema: Organization and WebSite on the homepage; SoftwareApplication on the homepage and each tool; HowTo for the visible steps; FAQPage only for FAQs actually on the page; BreadcrumbList on tools and guides. Keep JSON-LD in sync with visible text.
Internal links: homepage and /tools to all tools; each tool to 3 to 5 related tools; guides link in the body to the live tool, not only in a banner; breadcrumbs Home > Tools > Tool name.
hreflang reserved for a later language launch. Do not add hreflang until translated pages exist. Use /{lang}/slug when they do, with a self, peer, and x-default set.
Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verified, sitemap submitted, and domain property preferred in GSC. Set a US-English target, but expect global traffic on these queries.
Analytics that fits a privacy brand: if you use GA4, load it with consent. A first-party or privacy-oriented analytics tool is a better match. No third-party ad trackers on tool pages if the promise is that files and behavior stay private.
Performance budget: tool pages should still be useful on a mid-range phone. Compress and cache the engine. Offer a clear error when a file is too large for the browser tab.
Security and trust pages: /security should accurately describe in-browser processing. Do not copy competitors' "deleted in two hours" language if the file never arrived. /privacy and /terms must match the architecture. Unlock-PDF copy must require the user's own password.
Accessible forms and uploader (labels, keyboard, focus). Descriptive button names such as Compress PDF, not Submit.
Open Graph and Twitter titles that match the SEO title closely, plus a simple share image per tool or one high-quality default.

Launch and link building
Rankings on these terms follow links and unique product usefulness more than on-page copy. Nothing below should be posted until the user approves the exact text and destination. Prefer places where a no-upload, no-account toolkit is genuinely news.
1
Ship a live, fast product before any outreach
Product Hunt and most directories want a working site, not a waitlist. The first 5 tools should be polished, the privacy claim should be demonstrably true (no file POST in the Network tab), and /security, /privacy, and /terms should be accurate.
2
Launch on Product Hunt
Have the product live. Prepare a short tagline, a clear first comment that explains the in-browser difference and asks for feedback, gallery screenshots of 2 or 3 tools, and a 240 px thumbnail. Recent launch guides (for example https://spreadcast.app/blog/how-to-launch-on-product-hunt) recommend a personal hunter account that is not brand-new and a midnight Pacific launch window; treat that as community advice, not a guarantee. Do not buy votes or arrange reciprocal voting rings.
3
List on AlternativeTo and similar directories
Submit as an alternative to iLovePDF, Smallpdf, and PDF24, with a factual description of in-browser processing. AlternativeTo's FAQ is at https://alternativeto.net/faq/. Add other relevant directories only when the listing is accurate (free vs freemium).
4
Show HN and privacy or productivity communities, with restraint
A Show HN post is fitting if the architecture is real and the write-up is technical, not marketing fluff. On Reddit and similar forums, read the current subreddit rules before posting. Communities that sometimes welcome a genuinely useful free tool include privacy, productivity, and internet-is-beautiful type subs; many large subs ban self-promotion outright. Do not astroturf, do not post the same link everywhere, and do not use a brand-new account as a billboard. Open-sourcing the client would make an r/opensource or GitHub audience more receptive.
5
Answer real questions on Quora, Reddit, and forums
Target questions such as how to compress a PDF to 1 MB, how to merge without an account, or whether iLovePDF uploads files. Answer the question first. Link once, when it actually helps. The same applies to device-specific how-tos (iPhone sign or merge).
6
Pitch fair listicles and comparison authors
Several 2026 "best free PDF tools" roundups already compare free-tier limits and, in some cases, uploads (examples: https://trencada.com/pdf-tools-benchmark-2026/, https://comparetools.io/best-free-online-pdf-tools/, https://startupperks.co/best/free-pdf-editors, https://www.actuallyusefulextensions.com/blog/smallpdf-vs-ilovepdf-vs-convert-anything-to-pdf/). A short, factual note that a no-upload free toolkit now exists is a legitimate pitch. Do not pay for fake reviews or guaranteed placements.
7
Build a small, truthful comparison and security page that others can cite
A sourced page on who uploads, who caps free use, and how to verify in-browser processing can attract links if it stays accurate and dated. Update it when vendors change plans.
8
Later, not week one
Browser extension, Product Hunt ship updates, guest posts, campus or job-seeker communities, and language versions. International search demand for these tools is larger than US demand on the volume reports, but the first site should be excellent in English before translating.
Realistic expectations
No rankings or dates are promised
This brief does not include a timeline to page one, a promised traffic number, or a claim that long-tail pages will rank. Those would be invented. Head terms such as merge pdf, compress pdf, and pdf to word are held by long-standing domains with strong brands and backlink profiles. A new site usually wins first, if it wins, on distinctive pages (no upload, no sign-up, compress to 1 MB) and on links from launch coverage, not by slightly rewriting the same title tags the incumbents already use.
SEO results depend on the domain you register, existing history or penalties, technical quality, unique usefulness, and links. A better on-page brief cannot overcome a weak product or zero links.
PDF to Word, PDF to Excel, and OCR will not take share on claims alone. If conversion quality is poor, users bounce, and that hurts the whole domain.
In-browser processing has real limits (very large files, some conversion fidelity, heavy OCR). State them. Offer a clearly labeled path only if you later add a server option, and never silently upload after promising the opposite.
Re-check every competitor limit and every title character count before pages go live. Vendors change free tiers often.
Pick a name that can be trademark-cleared and that is not a typo of iLovePDF or Smallpdf. This brief leaves naming to the user.

Instructions for Claude: build the first version of a free online PDF toolkit with SEO baked in.

Product position
- Name and domain are still TBD. Use [Brand] in visible copy and do not hard-code a fake brand in URLs.
- Promise: free PDF tools that run in the browser. Files never leave the user's device. No account, no daily or hourly task cap, no watermark.
- Prove the promise: processing must happen client-side (for example pdf-lib, pdf.js, or WASM). After the page loads, a file operation must not POST the PDF to a server. Document this on /security and tell the user how to verify (Network tab or airplane mode after load).
- Do not invent user counts, accuracy rates, testimonials, awards, or "trusted by millions" lines.
- Do not copy iLovePDF, Smallpdf, or Adobe wording. Especially do not reuse claims like "almost 100% accurate."
- Unlock PDF may only remove a password the user supplies. Do not market password cracking.
- If a tool cannot yet meet a quality bar (PDF to Word, PDF to Excel, OCR), ship a useful limited v1 and say the limits in plain language on the page. Prefer delaying OCR rather than shipping a misleading converter.

- English site at the domain root. Kebab-case slugs, no .html, no underscores.
- Required routes: /, /tools, /compress-pdf, /compress-pdf-to-1mb, /merge-pdf, /split-pdf, /organize-pdf, /pdf-to-word, /word-to-pdf, /pdf-to-jpg, /jpg-to-pdf, /pdf-to-excel, /sign-pdf, /edit-pdf, /rotate-pdf, /unlock-pdf, /protect-pdf, /blog, /privacy, /terms, /security, /about.

The PDF engine can be client-only and should be lazy-loaded after first paint.

- Prepare Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verification (DNS or file). Add privacy-respecting analytics; if GA4 is used, it must be consent-gated. No ad or retargeting pixels on tool pages.

Product features to prioritize in the widget
- Compress: presets plus a target-size control (1 MB, 500 KB, 200 KB). Show resulting size before download.
- Merge: multi-file, drag to reorder.
- Split: range, every page, or every N pages.
- Sign: draw, type, or image; self-sign only in v1; do not claim certified e-sign legal status.
- Edit v1 may be overlays (text, highlight, ink, image, whiteout) if true text editing is not ready; say so.
- JPG to PDF should accept PNG and WebP as well.
- Organize combines reorder, delete, and rotate so we do not launch three thin pages.

Build order
1) App shell, homepage, /tools, /security, /privacy, /terms, sitemap, robots, analytics hook, schema helpers.
2) Compress, Merge, Split, Rotate, Organize, Protect, Unlock, JPG to PDF, PDF to JPG (strong in-browser fits).
3) Sign, Edit (overlay v1), Word to PDF, Compress to 1 MB.
4) PDF to Word and PDF to Excel with honest quality limits.
5) Blog templates and the first two guides: compress to 1 MB, and "does this tool upload my file?"

Out of scope for v1 unless quality is clearly ready: OCR, batch server workflows, accounts, ads, watermarked outputs, paid paywalls on core tools.
